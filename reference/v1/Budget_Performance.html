Did cutting the budget cost us anything?

The answer is no — the cut did not cost us revenue. We spent $189,338 less and brought in $8,276 less in first-month revenue. Cost per new customer fell from $591 to $306, and every dollar now returns $3.46 instead of $1.48. On these numbers the 2025 spend level was substantially inefficient, and the reduction removed waste rather than demand.

What the cut did cost us is volume: 105 fewer new customers. That matters over years, not months, because those customers are the base that compounds. The workbook's finding that a 2010–2012 account is worth 20× a 2025 account is the reason volume still deserves attention even when efficiency looks this good.

Monthly spend — the April cliff

The timing pattern matters more than the total. Spend was actually up year-over-year in January (+54%) and March (+101%), then fell 78–90% every month from April onward. The cut was not gradual — it was a cliff in April. That is why the M1 revenue comparison holds up: the strong revenue months of May and June 2026 came after the spend had already been cut, which is difficult to reconcile with a story where spend drives near-term revenue.

What we recommend for the second half of the year

Three actions the CMO is asking leadership to greenlight.

If July's ~$10K/month pace holds, 2026 will land at roughly $187K vs the $291K plan — 36% under for the full year, or about $104K of headroom. That's meaningful capital available to be deployed in the remaining five months or held as savings. Three specific actions worth discussing:
  • Re-engage Google paid search. At just 25% of the annual budget spent through July ($21K of $87K), we have significant room to increase Google spend. Note that Google absorbed 47% of the entire year-over-year reduction on its own — $89,034 of the $189,338 — so this is where the cut was concentrated, not where it was spread.
  • Fund the photo & video production. $15K was budgeted, $300 has been spent. This work is planned for the back half of the year and the money is still available.
  • Correct the SEO booking. The $9,454 charged to VHPC for the missed Scorpion posting should be moved to GarageExperts. Once reclassified, our real year-to-date spend drops by roughly $9,500 — improving the efficiency picture even further.
