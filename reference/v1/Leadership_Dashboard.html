Why Organic & Untracked matter: Together they represent 73% of our customers (646 of 885) and 55% of M1 revenue ($569K of $1.04M) — more than all paid channels combined. They don't have direct spend attached, but they're not "free":
  • Organic Search is influenced by historical SEO investments, content production, technical site health, and brand awareness built by paid channels.
  • Untracked records are customers whose source we never captured — a mix of direct, word-of-mouth, and returning customers. There has been a sustained loss in tracking information — reducing this bucket should be a marketing operations priority.
TL;DR: "Revenue per $ Spent" is the simplest way to read paid performance. $1.00 means we're breaking even. Above $1 = making money; below $1 = losing money in Month 1. When you combine paid + earned + untracked, every $1 spent on marketing returns $2.25 in Month 1 revenue (see OVERALL row).

Could be summer seasonality (contractor slow season) or algorithm-driven auction dynamics. Watch August closely — this is the pattern we most need to see reverse or hold steady.

🔵
Google Ads now bids higher on new customers — changed July 27We raised the value assigned to a new customer inside Google's bidding model, so the algorithm bids more aggressively on first-time buyers instead of spreading budget across returning traffic. This went live around July 27, which means only four days of July sit behind it — July's numbers above do not reflect the change. August is the first clean read.
🔵
80% of Google budget is now in Performance Max — being evaluated in AugustPerformance Max has been delivering more conversions at lower cost than our search campaigns, so we moved the majority of Google budget into it. We are evaluating through August to confirm it is still working. If it stops performing, the plan is to move new-user acquisition back to a search-based campaign and reserve Performance Max for retargeting recent purchasers — which pairs directly with the $1K retargeting ask on the initiatives dashboard.
