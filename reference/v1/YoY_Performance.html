What to watch on the next refresh: the May and June 2026 cohorts were exceptional months ($87,841 and $130,063 first-month NET). Whether they keep spending at that rate through the autumn is what determines if 2026 is a genuinely better program or a well-timed one.

Who our customers are (data quality note)

The categorization is cleaner this year

Contractor customers (professional installers) vs DIY customers (homeowners installing themselves). The apparent shift year-over-year is a data-quality improvement, not a real change in the customer base.

January through July 2025

January through July 2026

Read this carefully — it's not a mix shift, it's cleaner data: The apparent move from 85% Contractor / 14% DIY to 78% Contractor / 22% DIY is not a change in who is buying from us. It reflects better categorization at the point of sale — the sales team has been more consistent in 2026 about tagging DIY customers correctly, so people who were previously mislabeled as Contractor (or left uncategorized and later defaulted) are now being recorded accurately. The underlying customer base has been more stable than these numbers make it look. Recommended next step: once we have a full clean year of 2026 data, we can re-baseline and start tracking a real year-over-year mix trend.

This is the finding that should change the marketing mandate. No realistic acquisition programme closes a 20× value gap. If we lose legacy accounts at the current rate, no volume of new customers replaces them — and the numbers earlier on this page show new customers arriving at 19% lower volume than last year. The implication is uncomfortable but clear: protecting the accounts we have is worth more than acquiring new ones, and marketing has almost no programme aimed at protection. Read the top block carefully. 466 of 1,110 established accounts that had revenue in the prior year — 42% — bought nothing in the trailing twelve months. Yet retained-account revenue rose from $14.1M to $17.5M. Revenue held because the survivors spent more, not because the base was stable. That is a concentration risk: the same $17.5M now rests on 644 accounts instead of 1,110.

The workbook dates the onset of this to May–June 2025 — at peak advertising spend, which rules out the budget as a cause. Returns were also ruled out (under $2,032/month). Rep coverage was restructured in summer 2025, which is the closest match on timing and the most plausible candidate. That is a correlation, not proof, and it is worth investigating directly before the next planning cycle.

What we recommend

What the numbers say to do next

Ordered by expected value against effort, using the findings on this page.

1 — Cheapest, highest value
Build a lapse alert
Flag any account exceeding twice its own median order interval. One report. It would have caught Welborn Garage Doors (164 orders every 3 days) within a fortnight instead of 223 days, and would have surfaced the mid-2025 retention break as it started. This is the single best return on effort available.
2 — Concentrated and named
Work the 28-account call list
$1.27M of the $2.78M at risk sits in 28 named accounts. Start with the GRANITE cluster ($282k across 3) and the two high-frequency accounts. This is a call list for sales, not a campaign — and it should be the first target for the dormant email flow once its manual push runs.
3 — Two conversations, not ten
Escalate MACH ONE and GRANITE
Six of the ten largest declining accounts belong to these two networks. MACH ONE is down 24% and GRANITE 17% at network level. Losses inside a network usually share one cause, so these should be network-level conversations with whoever makes purchasing decisions.
4 — Reframe the mandate
Account development over volume
A 20× value gap between legacy and new accounts cannot be closed by acquisition. Marketing currently has four email flows aimed at retention, two of which have never fired. That is the entire retention programme. It needs to be the priority, not the side project.
5 — Investigate, don't assume
Confirm the summer-2025 cause
Order frequency began decaying May–June 2025, at peak ad spend. Rep coverage was restructured that summer, which matches on timing — but correlation is not proof. Returns are already ruled out. Worth confirming directly before the next planning cycle, because the answer changes what we fix.
6 — Redeploy available budget
$65,630 unspent on Google
Google has $65,630 left against the annual plan and photo/video is almost untouched at $300 of a $30K line. The focus group asked specifically for real-garage installation video. Two underspent lines, one obvious use.
The single message for leadership: The in-year marketing engine is working — spend down 58%, revenue essentially flat, acquisition cost halved. But the numbers that matter more are structural: a legacy account is worth 20× a new one, 42% of established accounts stopped buying in the last twelve months, and $2.78M sits in accounts that have gone quiet. No budget level fixes those. The mandate needs to shift from acquisition volume to account development and retention — and today marketing has almost no programme aimed at either.
